**First: convergent HCDR-2 improbable mutations drive V3-glycan broadly neutralizing antibody maturation across primates.**

One of the central problems in H-I-V vaccine design is that the broadly neutralizing antibodies capable of neutralizing diverse H-I-V strains carry a specific class of mutations — mutations that are statistically unlikely to arise during normal B cell affinity maturation, called improbable mutations — and understanding exactly which mutations are the load-bearing ones is the structural foundation for designing immunogens that can selectively recruit and mature those lineages. A study published today in Science Translational Medicine, from Wilton Williams, Kevin Wiehe, and Barton Haynes at Duke, with Beatrice Hahn and George Shaw at Penn and Bette Korber at Los Alamos, provides a striking data point in that program: a convergent improbable mutation in heavy chain complementarity determining region two appears independently in both human and macaque broadly neutralizing antibody lineages targeting the same V3-glycan epitope supersite on the H-I-V envelope.

The study follows eighteen rhesus macaques infected with a Simian-H-I-V bearing the BG505 transmitted-founder envelope, a prototypic subtype A strain used widely in vaccine research. Three of those macaques developed V3-glycan broadly neutralizing antibodies, but only after more than one year of infection. From the macaque with the strongest breadth, the authors isolated two hundred and three members of a single clonal lineage they call DH1030. When they compared DH1030 to DH270 — the human V3-glycan broadly neutralizing antibody lineage isolated from a person with subtype C H-I-V infection — the structural and genetic similarity was striking: both lineages independently evolved a glycine-to-arginine mutation at almost the same position in heavy chain CDR-2, position 56 in the macaque and position 57 in the human. Reverting that arginine back to glycine in either lineage dramatically reduces or abolishes heterologous neutralization. Structurally, the arginine makes direct contact with the G-D-I-R peptide motif at the base of the third variable loop of Env — it is the structural element that allows the antibody to displace the Env V1 loop and access the glycan supersite at N332. This convergent pattern holds across two different primate species, two distinct viral subtypes — subtype A in the macaque host, subtype C in the human — and two completely independent infections.

The paper goes further into the cellular mechanism of how this mutation is selected. Using a single-cell approach they call PheBAR-seq — which combines antigen-specific B cell sorting with paired B cell receptor and transcriptome sequencing from peripheral blood, lymph node, and spleen — they track which B cells in the DH1030 lineage acquire this arginine, where those cells reside, and what additional mutations follow. The key finding is that DH1030 B cells bearing the G56R improbable mutation are predominantly found in lymph node memory subsets, while the germline-like G56 cells are retained in the germinal center. The improbable mutation appears to be what drives exit from the germinal center into memory: it is simultaneously necessary for neutralization breadth and necessary for positive selection out of the germinal center. A second mutation, serine-to-proline at position 114 in heavy chain CDR-3, appears as a compensatory mutation only after G56R is fixed and acts to bring a neighboring aspartate residue into hydrogen-bonding distance with the N332 glycan — a further structural accommodation that broadens the repertoire of Envs the antibody can neutralize.

The vaccine-design implication of this convergence is specific. The data strengthen the case that SHIV-infected macaques faithfully model the cellular and structural constraints on human bnAb development — two species targeting distinct envelope subtypes arrive at the same improbable mutation because the V3-glycan epitope imposes the same structural requirement on any antibody that occupies it. This means vaccine immunogens need to be designed not just to elicit V3-glycan binders in general, but to select specifically for this arginine in CDR-2. The authors show that V1-glycan-deleted Env variants appear to prime both the human DH270 and the macaque DH1030 lineages — an observation consistent with ongoing human clinical trials testing V1-deleted BG505-based immunogens.

Paper link: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.aee9864

**Second: a tandem bispecific interleukin-7 receptor agonist antibody selectively expands CD4 memory T cells.**

Interleukin-7 is a critical cytokine for T cell homeostasis, and there have been long-standing clinical interests in using it to restore T cell numbers in lymphopenic conditions. The challenge is that recombinant interleukin-7 has a short half-life and immunogenicity risks. A paper from the Yea group at the Daegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology in Korea, published today in Protein Engineering, Design and Selection, reports the engineering of a bispecific antibody called T-B-4 that functions as a fully human interleukin-7 receptor agonist by simultaneously engaging interleukin-7 receptor alpha and the common gamma chain.

The key engineering choice is the format. Rather than the knobs-into-holes bispecific architecture commonly used for bispecific antibodies, T-B-4 uses a tetravalent tandem single-chain variable fragment F-c architecture — four antigen-binding domains arranged in series within a single F-c scaffold. The domain orientation and linker length were optimized to achieve cis-engagement of both receptor subunits on the same cell rather than bridging two separate cells, and to prevent unintended gamma chain crosslinking between complexes. Compared to the knobs-into-holes bispecific control, this format delivers markedly enhanced binding affinity and avidity. At the cell level, T-B-4 reduces internalization of the engaged receptor complex, which translates into sustained STAT5 phosphorylation in primary human T cells — a key downstream signaling output of the interleukin-7 receptor. In terms of selectivity, T-B-4 preferentially expands CD4-positive memory subsets, and specifically effector memory cells that have re-expressed CD45RA, rather than the broader T cell expansion that native interleukin-7 drives. Transcriptomic profiling distinguishes T-B-4 from the natural cytokine further: T-B-4 uniquely activates an antiviral and innate immune gene signature in expanded T cells on top of the shared proliferative program. The engineering lesson here is that for bispecific agonists of heterodimeric cytokine receptor complexes, the format — valency, geometry, and linker length — directly tunes both activation magnitude and cell-type specificity, and this paper provides a concrete case where those design variables translate into distinct biology. Paper link: https://doi.org/10.1093/protein/gzag017
